Introduction

Research Background & Objectives

Background

  • Shopping is a key daily activity for developing student independence.
  • Students are exposed to diverse product choices, prices, promotions, and social media trends.
  • Spending decisions reflect personal value assessments beyond total money available.

Specific Objectives

  • Identify GMIS students' spending patterns & shopping frequency.
  • Measure the level of influence across financial, product, personal, and social factors.
  • Determine the factors with the greatest and least influence.
🎙️ Speaker Note: As students become more independent, their spending decisions involve balancing financial awareness with marketing triggers and personal preferences.
Framework

Research Methodology

Research Design

Quantitative research design using structured numerical analysis and descriptive statistics.

Respondents

63 Middle & Senior Students across Grades 6 to 12 at GMIS Semarang.

Instrument & Analysis

Google Forms questionnaire measuring habits, spending levels, and a 5-point influence scale.

Overall Factor Scale Rating

Across the 5-point scale, most individual factors rated between slight influence and moderate influence, indicating student spending is shaped by a balanced combination of factors rather than a single dominant trigger.

🎙️ Speaker Note: Almost 43% of students demonstrate smart shopping habits by regularly comparing prices before purchasing.
Summary

Conclusion

1. Multi-Factor Driven

Student expenditure is shaped by a mix of available budget, pricing, promotions, personal preferences, and practical necessity.

2. Budget Primacy

The total amount of money available remains the primary factor determining total shopping expenditure.

3. Impulse Overruns

Unplanned purchases lead over half (52.4%) of students to exceed their planned shopping budget.

🎙️ Speaker Note: In conclusion, while budget constraints set the outer limits of spending, unplanned purchases frequently push students past their initial budgets.
Actionable Steps

Recommendations

For Students & Parents

  • Students: Set strict spending limits and prepare shopping lists to minimize impulse buying.
  • Parents: Discuss home budgeting and guide students in distinguishing "needs" vs. "wants."

For School & Researchers

  • School Community: Offer workshops on practical financial literacy and consumer awareness.
  • Future Researchers: Expand sample size across schools and study social media trends in greater depth.
